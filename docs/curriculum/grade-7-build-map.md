# Grade 7 instructional build map

**Status:** scope and sequence for authoring, not a claim that the full year has been published. The existing catalog contains starter lessons in seven Grade 7 subjects. Unit 1 of Entrepreneurship & Financial Independence is the first authored pilot in this map.

## Learning model

- Four academic quarters, approximately nine instructional weeks each. Each course needs a diagnostic, explicit teaching, worked examples, guided and independent practice, feedback, section assessment, and a final application.
- Grade 7 reading, writing, research, proportional reasoning, and financial literacy form a bridge to high-school work. Business tasks apply math and communication skills; they do not replace core subjects.
- Each unit should include at least one human-graded product with a rubric. Multiple-choice checks diagnose knowledge; a portfolio demonstrates transfer.
- Teachers can hold live lessons and office hours for their assigned grade. A student can complete the asynchronous path when a live meeting is missed; equivalent work must be available.
- Collect only necessary student work and avoid asking minors to solicit strangers, publish private information, sign contracts, or open financial accounts.

## External benchmarks and Prosper design choices

- [Stanford Online High School teaching model](https://onlinehighschool.stanford.edu/about/school-design-pedagogy): students prepare with materials before live, interactive seminars. Prosper can use the same *principle* by making the readable lesson and practice complete before a teacher-led business lab; we are not reproducing Stanford's curriculum or claiming its staffing level.
- [ASU Prep Digital middle school](https://www.asuprepdigital.org/online-middle-school-program/): live teaching is paired with success-coach support. Prosper's teacher sessions and grade-targeted alerts are a starting point; a real advising routine and response-time commitment still need people and operating procedures.
- [Laurel Springs self-paced model](https://laurelsprings.com/best-k-12-online-school/): students can work asynchronously with teacher and counselor support. Prosper should let students learn without attending a live class while keeping feedback and human help available.
- [Jump$tart/CEE personal finance standards](https://www.jumpstart.org/education/national-standards/): earning, spending, saving, investing, managing credit, and managing risk provide a useful progression. Age-appropriate Grade 7 entrepreneurship should start with evidence, unit economics, budgeting, and ethics; advanced financial topics come later.

These are product observations from the schools' and standard setter's own descriptions. They do not establish comparative learning outcomes or accreditation for Prosper Prep.

## Unit standard for every course

1. Begin with a real problem or primary text and a short diagnostic.
2. Teach the concept with precise examples and common misconceptions.
3. Offer practice with feedback before a graded check.
4. Assess each section with new transfer questions; do not recycle the lesson examples verbatim.
5. Require a written or practical product evaluated with a published rubric.
6. Reassess after feedback, keep a record of attempts, and show progress to families.

## First pilot: entrepreneurship, quarter 1

The three authored lessons in `prisma/grade7-enterprise.ts` teach evidence-based problem discovery, unit economics, and honest offer testing. They include original cases, independent work, six graded questions per lesson, a twelve-question section quiz, and a teacher-scored evidence portfolio in lesson three. The remaining Grade 7 entrepreneurship lessons are still starter content and require the same authoring pass. A full course also needs more guided feedback and sustained student work.
