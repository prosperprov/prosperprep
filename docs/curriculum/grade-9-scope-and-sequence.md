## Shared instructional model (every core course)

| Element | Weight (suggested) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson checks (MCQ + short written) | 25% | Teach-first lesson → short response required on key lessons |
| Section / unit quizzes | 20% | Mix MCQ + 1–2 constructed responses |
| Labs / projects / essays | 25% | Rubric-scored; originality enforced |
| Midterm | 15% | Cumulative half-course |
| Final | 15% | Cumulative; includes performance task |

**Beyond multiple choice (required patterns):**

- **Short constructed response (SCR):** 3–8 sentences, claim + evidence
- **Extended writing:** timed essay or multi-draft paper
- **Labs / investigations:** procedure, data, analysis, error discussion
- **Projects:** research brief, presentation outline, or design challenge
- **Live / teacher-led:** discussion prompts or optional live-session outline per unit (asynchronous forum acceptable if live not scheduled)

**Licensing rule for authors:** Original Prosper Prep prose. External media only if CC BY or public domain (OpenStax CC BY editions, PhET CC BY sims, Library of Congress / Wikimedia PD images). No Eureka NC, no Khan rehost, no CK-12 NC items inside paid platform without separate license.

## 1. English 9: Literature & Composition

**Standards:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL/RI/W/SL/L.9–10  
**Year theme:** Voice, evidence, and argument — reading complex texts; writing with claims and citations.

### Units (36 weeks / 8 units)

| # | Unit | Focus texts (PD/CC BY or original excerpts + LOCs) | Weeks | Culminating work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reading like a scholar | Short fiction + nonfiction craft | 4 | Annotated passage + SCR claim |
| 2 | Narrative craft | Memoir / coming-of-age | 4 | Personal narrative (multi-draft) |
| 3 | Argument foundations | Op-eds, speeches (PD) | 5 | Argument essay (claim/counterclaim) |
| 4 | Drama & dialogue | Public-domain play (e.g. Shakespeare scene set) | 4 | Scene analysis + performance script notes |
| 5 | Poetry & precision | PD poetry set | 3 | Close-reading portfolio |
| 6 | Research & information literacy | Multi-source inquiry | 5 | Research brief (MLA) + annotated bib |
| 7 | Novel study | One PD novel or novella | 6 | Literary analysis essay |
| 8 | Synthesis & SAT/ACT reading preview | Mixed passages | 5 | Midyear/final style synthesis task |

**Assessments:** Unit quizzes (MCQ+SCR); midterm (units 1–4); final (full year + timed essay).  
**Live / discussion prompts (sample per unit type):**

- U1: “What evidence in the text would survive a skeptic’s challenge?”
- U3: “Steel-man the opposing view before you rebut it.”
- U6: “Which source is least reliable here—and how do you know?”
- U7: “How does the author’s structure create meaning, not just plot?”

**Feeds G10:** Analytical essay stamina → English 10 World/American lit; ACT/SAT reading & writing foundations.

## 2. Algebra I

**Standards:** CCSS Algebra I (HSN, HSA, HSF, HSS as applicable)  
**Placement:** Diagnostic; Geometry track students use parallel Geometry S&S (see appendix note).

### Units

| # | Unit | Topics | Weeks | Culminating work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Expressions & equations | Simplify, solve linear | 4 | Error-analysis SCR set |
| 2 | Inequalities & modeling | 1-var / compound | 3 | Real-world model write-up |
| 3 | Functions & graphs | Function notation, domain | 4 | Function gallery project |
| 4 | Linear systems | Graph, substitution, elimination | 4 | Systems modeling mini-lab |
| 5 | Exponents & polynomials | Ops, factoring intro | 5 | Skills portfolio |
| 6 | Quadratics intro | Graphs, factoring, formula | 5 | Projectile / area lab report |
| 7 | Data & descriptive stats | Scatter, lines of best fit | 4 | Data investigation |
| 8 | Cumulative problem solving | Mixed ACT-style | 3 | Performance task + review |

**Assessments:** Section quizzes; midterm after U4; final with calculator & non-calculator parts; 2–3 SCR “explain the method” items every unit.  
**Live prompts:** “Why does this solution fail the original equation?” · “Which representation (table/graph/equation) is most efficient here?”  
**Feeds G10:** Geometry (proof readiness + coordinate) or Algebra II; ACT Math algebra fluency.

## 3. Biology

**Standards:** NGSS HS-LS1–LS4 (+ science practices SEP, CCC)  
**Lab safety:** Required module before wet/sim labs.

### Units

| # | Unit | NGSS focus | Weeks | Culminating work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Science practices & safety | SEP | 1 | Safety certification quiz |
| 1 | Chemistry of life | LS1 | 3 | Macromolecule claim SCR |
| 2 | Cells & organelles | LS1 | 4 | Cell analogy project + quiz |
| 3 | Energy & metabolism | LS1/LS2 | 4 | Enzyme lab report (PhET/sim OK if CC BY) |
| 4 | DNA → protein | LS1/LS3 | 4 | Central dogma model + SCR |
| 5 | Cell cycle & genetics | LS3 | 5 | Punnett / pedigree case study |
| 6 | Evolution & biodiversity | LS4 | 5 | Evidence-of-evolution brief |
| 7 | Ecology & human impact | LS2 | 5 | Ecosystem energy project |
| 8 | Capstone | Synthesis | 3 | Open-inquiry investigation |

**Assessments:** Lab reports (min. 4 formal); unit quizzes; midterm; final with practical data analysis.  
**Live prompts:** “What would falsify this hypothesis?” · “Where does this model break down in a real organism?”  
**Feeds G10:** Chemistry; later Anatomy/AP Bio path; ACT Science data literacy.

## 4. World History I: Civilizations to ~1750

**Standards:** C3 Framework (D1–D4); literacy in history/SS (CCSS RH/WHST 9–10); Texas-friendly civics threads (governance, rights, civic virtue) without East-Texas-only framing.  
**Pending:** Confirm vs World Geography.

### Units

| # | Unit | Focus | Weeks | Culminating work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Historical thinking | Sources, bias, chronology | 3 | Source analysis SCR |
| 2 | Early river civilizations | Mesopotamia, Egypt, Indus, China | 4 | Comparative gov project |
| 3 | Classical Mediterranean & Asia | Greece, Rome, Persia, India, Han | 5 | Civic virtue / citizenship essay |
| 4 | Belief systems & culture | Major traditions (academic, not confessional) | 4 | Comparative belief chart + SCR |
| 5 | Medieval worlds | Europe, Islamic caliphates, Africa, Americas | 5 | Trade-network map project |
| 6 | Encounters & exchange | Mongols, Indian Ocean, Silk Roads | 4 | Continuity/change essay |
| 7 | Early modern transitions | Renaissance, Reformation, empires to 1750 | 5 | DBQ-style mini |
| 8 | Capstone | Theme across eras | 2 | Portfolio defense (live or recorded) |

**Assessments:** Map quizzes; SCR source work; midterm; final DBQ-lite.  
**Live prompts:** “Whose voice is missing from this source?” · “What problem was this government trying to solve?”  
**Feeds G10:** World History II / Modern World or U.S. History; AP Human Geo / AP World readiness.

## Progression path (Grade 9 → 12 → college)

```
G9: Eng 9 + Alg I (or Geo) + Biology + World Hist I
        ↓
G10: Eng 10 + Geometry/Alg II + Chemistry + World Hist II or US Hist
        ↓
G11: Eng 11 + Alg II/Precalc + Physics or Anat + US Hist / Gov
        ↓
G12: Eng 12 / Comp + Precalc/Calc/Stats + Science elective + Econ/Gov + senior capstone
```

**College readiness outcomes by end of G9:**

- Write a multi-paragraph argument with cited evidence
- Solve linear and introductory quadratic problems with written reasoning
- Design a simple controlled investigation and interpret a data table/graph
- Analyze a primary source for purpose, audience, and bias
- Baseline ACT/SAT skill map started (reading stamina, algebra fluency)

**Athletics / entrepreneurship hooks (light touch in cores):** time-management modules; one Eng unit prompt on sports journalism or NIL literacy (age-appropriate); one Alg data project on training metrics — full Athletics & Entrepreneurship electives remain separate tracks.
