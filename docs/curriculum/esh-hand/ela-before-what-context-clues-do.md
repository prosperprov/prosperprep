## Objective

**I can** use surrounding words to infer meaning of unfamiliar vocabulary.

**Teacher focus:** Students explain *why* a choice works (a word, a sentence, a piece of evidence), not only label it. Prefer complete sentences on exit tickets.

## Warm-up (3–5 minutes)

Circle any unfamiliar word in this sentence and guess its meaning from neighbors: “The ranger’s concise warning — short, clear, and urgent — kept hikers from cutting the muddy switchback.”

Turn and talk: Which earlier habit from Unit 1 might help today?

## Teach

### Big idea

Strong readers do not stop at “I don’t know that word.” They hunt for **context clues** and **word parts** (roots, prefixes, suffixes). Today’s skill — **What Context Clues Do** — builds that detective habit.

### Why it matters

At Prosper Prep, clear reading and writing support scholarship habits: follow directions, cite evidence, and revise until a stranger can follow your thinking. Families use these skills when reading news, contracts, team emails, and faith or community texts. East Texas students deserve elite literacy tools — not tricks.

### Language bank

- **Context clue** = hints in the same sentence or nearby sentences.
- **Root / affix** = word parts that carry meaning across many words.
- **Connotation** = the feeling a word carries beyond its dictionary sense.

### Worked example A

Imagine a practice for the spring showcase. Related practice numbers/labels you may see in items: set 10, set 4.

**Mentor passage (original Prosper Prep):**

> Maya paused at the mailbox. The envelope was thin, but her name looked official. She did not open it on the porch. Inside, she set it on the kitchen table and washed her hands first, as if cleanliness could calm the shaking in her fingers. When she finally slid a thumb under the flap, she found three sentences and a deadline. Hope, she realized, could be heavy.

**Step 1 — Restate the task** in your own words (main idea? craft? integration?).
**Step 2 — Annotate:** underline claims, circle key words, star evidence.
**Step 3 — Answer with a complete sentence** that includes a pointer to the text.
**Step 4 — Check:** Could a partner find your evidence in under 10 seconds?

**Sample response quality:** “The central idea is that careful hikers protect trails by slowing down at muddy markers, which is supported by the detail that most injuries happen when people rush the last half mile.”

### Worked example B (different structure)

Keep the skill of “What Context Clues Do,” but switch the context to a Prosper Prep morning advisory. Use a fresh short example (numbers 4 and 23 if you invent a list or ranking).

1. Write a one-sentence goal.
2. Show the markings (parts of speech, clues, or annotations).
3. Produce the answer or revision.
4. Add a concluding sentence that names the skill.

Teachers: freeze after step 2 in live sessions so students cannot hide behind premature answers.

### Common mistakes

- **Watch for:** switching verb tense mid-paragraph for no reason.
- **Also watch for:** copying a whole paragraph as “evidence” instead of selecting the precise line, or fixing grammar by making sentences longer and muddier.
- **Repair move:** Name the broken step in one sentence, then revise only from that step.

### Connect to prior learning

Vocabulary and key-idea reading feed each other: you cannot summarize well if key words stay foggy.
